\begin{abstract}
Formal Contract-Driven Development (FCDD) organises an AI coding agent's work
around a machine-checked contract. Its purpose is \emph{predictability}: a
repair pinned to a kernel-checked specification should land in a narrow band
of behaviour and cost, and should not drift into hallucinated or unfounded
changes. This study asks two questions in that order: does the pinning work,
and what does it cost? We ran a controlled comparison on a 7{,}343-line Z80
assembly chess engine into which an independent agent seeded seven
single-byte faults, each with a sealed acceptance test written before any
repair was attempted; two arms fixed every fault. \emph{On cost the answer is
unambiguous}: FCDD was more expensive on 7 of 7 matched pairs (exact
two-sided sign test $p=0.016$, the study's only statistically supportable
result), median ratio $4.74\times$ on raw tokens (the pre-registered primary
metric), and its upfront contract never amortised. \emph{On predictability
the data is suggestive and insufficient}: the across-defect dispersion
contrast rests entirely on one cell --- excluding it the two arms' spreads
are identical at $2.31\times$ --- and replicating that cell showed the
ordinary arm forking between a minimal repair (\$11.78) and a
below-gate-floor redesign (\$44.27, \$41.35) while FCDD produced the minimal
repair in all three runs, at a replication depth where no dispersion
statistic can reach significance (Fisher $p=0.40$; attainable floor $0.10$).
The quality comparison's paired CI, $[-0.44,+0.78]$, spans zero. The honest
summary is asymmetric: the price is demonstrated; the product is not yet. We
also report three measurement findings we regard as more transferable than
either ratio: an instruction to review ``until no findings remain'' has no
fixed point; measured cost covaries with review-loop length at least as
strongly as with method; and processes killed by infrastructure emit no cost
record, under-counting one cell by roughly $20\times$.

Predictability \and LLM agents \and Cost measurement \and Code review}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Agentic AI code repair has a predictability problem before it has a cost
problem. An unconstrained agent asked to fix a fault may fix it minimally, or
redesign the subsystem around it, or ``fix'' code that was never broken; runs
of the same task have been measured to differ by up to $30\times$ in token
consumption~\cite{bai2026}, and agent-reported success diverges widely from
human-confirmed success~\cite{rondon2025}. For a maintainer, the expected
cost of a fix matters less than the variance of what they will receive.

Formal Contract-Driven Development (FCDD) is a response to that problem, not
to the cost problem. It surrounds the agent with a machine-checked contract
--- a kernel-checked specification the fix must name, satisfy and extend ---
on the theory that a repair pinned to named clauses cannot silently become a
redesign, and a change with no clause behind it is visible as such. That is
the design intent. Whether the pinning \emph{works} is an empirical question,
and it is the first of the two questions this study asks:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Goal 1 --- predictability.} Does the contract make the agent's
repairs land in a narrower band --- of solution strategy, of quality, of cost
--- and does it reduce unfounded changes?
\item \textbf{Goal 2 --- cost.} What does the pinning cost, per defect,
against a competent ordinary alternative doing the identical repair to the
identical gate?
\end{itemize}

The intended thesis, stated before the data so the reader can watch it take
damage: \emph{predictability is a trade-off purchased with elevated cost} ---
the ordinary approach is cheap when the problem is easy but unpredictable and
occasionally very expensive, whereas the formal approach costs more up front
and lands in a narrow band. Section~\ref{sec:results} tests each half
separately. The cost half survives intact. The predictability half survives
only as a directional observation on one replicated defect, and we say so
plainly: this experiment was powered to price the method, not to prove its
purpose.

A second contribution is methodological. Three times a striking result proved
to be an artefact of our apparatus rather than a property of the methods; a
fourth and fifth were caught only by adversarial review of the finished
draft, and this revision added a sixth (a published regression that did not
reproduce; Sect.~\ref{sec:effort}). Each correction moved a headline, and two
reversed its direction. Section~\ref{sec:instrument} is therefore placed
before the results.

\section{The Method Under Test}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{The two arms}

\textbf{Ordinary development-plus-review (Arm~A).} Reproduce the symptom, fix
the fault, add a test, obtain a review from a fresh reviewer, address its
findings; stop when the pre-registered gate passes. No formal-methods
vocabulary appears in the instructions.

\textbf{FCDD (Arm~B).} The same bug report and gate, but the session works
around a pre-built machine-checked contract, following the lifecycle in
Fig.~\ref{fig:process}.

\subsection{The four beats and their artefacts}

FCDD builds the contract once, in four beats (Fig.~\ref{fig:process}, top).
(1)~\textsc{prove}: the safety properties are formalised as the \emph{spec of
record} in a kernel-checked prover --- here Lean~4 core, producing
\texttt{Contract.lean}: 1{,}271 lines, 95 theorems, zero \texttt{sorry},
empty axiom profile. (2)~\textsc{twin}: a \emph{pure twin implementation} ---
side-effect-free, no I/O --- transcribed clause-for-clause from the spec.
(3)~\textsc{bridge}: a \emph{conformance suite} connecting twin to spec and
to the real engine through the emulator: witnesses must conform, inputs that
violate a theorem must fail the \emph{right} clause, and single-field
mutations must trip exactly their clause. The bridge \emph{samples}
agreement; it is not a refinement proof, and the method says so.
(4)~\textsc{attack}: multi-agent adversarial review with distinct lenses,
findings verified by execution and fixed at the layer that caused them.

\paragraph{What tier of verification this actually is.} All 95 theorems are
discharged by \texttt{rfl} --- kernel evaluation of a closed decidable
Boolean on a concrete witness. There is no universally-quantified theorem, no
induction and no invariant proof in the contract. The tier is
\emph{kernel-evaluated finite-scope checking}: the Lean kernel used as a
trustworthy test runner. The consequence favours the paper's own cost
headline: we priced the cheapest formal tier, so our cost ratio is a
\emph{lower bound} on what deductive verification would have cost. The
artefact belongs with lightweight formal methods rather than with seL4-style
proof.

\subsection{How the contract constrains the repair agent}

The per-defect FCDD session (Fig.~\ref{fig:process}, middle) receives the
same symptom-level bug report as Arm~A, plus the contract package on disk.
Its instructions require it to: run the bridge against the faulty build and
\emph{locate the violated clause}; if no clause is violated, \emph{extend the
contract} until one is (every one of the seven cells did extend it, by $+12$
to $+35$ theorems; Sect.~\ref{sec:asymmetry}); fix at the offending layer;
\emph{re-prove} (the kernel gate must return green over all theorems, old and
new); re-run the \emph{bridge}; and submit to an \emph{attack round} of
independent reviewers, capped at three rounds like Arm~A's review.

This is the mechanism by which predictability \emph{could} arise, stated here
as design rather than result: a redesign must leave every named theorem
green, which prices deviation from specified behaviour; a change with no
clause behind it is structurally visible; and the seeded fault itself becomes
a permanent negative test. Whether any of that shows up in the data is the
question of Sects.~\ref{sec:dispersion} and~\ref{sec:quality}.

\subsection{What the method itself disclaims}

FCDD's own documentation states two residuals that bound everything this
study can conclude: the contract establishes \emph{coherence, never
correctness of intent} --- garbage clauses, kernel-checked, are still garbage
--- and the bridge cannot catch a \emph{common-mode error} in which
specification and twin share one author's misconception. Both residuals
became measurable events in this study (Sect.~\ref{sec:asymmetry}'s vacuous
antisymmetry clause; Sect.~\ref{sec:beyond}'s gate common-mode), so they are
not hypothetical.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Empirical cost of formal methods.} The field's own guidance notes
that empirical studies of formal methods are rare~\cite{terbeek2022}. What
exists is mostly retrospective single-project accounting with no control arm:
seL4 needed 2.2 person-years to build against about 20 to
verify~\cite{staples2014,klein2009}; ShardStore's authors compare their
overhead to figures \emph{drawn from other papers}~\cite{bornholt2021}; the
AWS TLA+ reports rest on counterfactual testimony~\cite{newcombe2015};
CompCert reports cost without comparison~\cite{leroy2009}. The largest survey
concedes the gap outright: in a majority of projects surveyed there is no
data available on relative time and costs~\cite{woodcock2009}. The classic
industrial comparison is observational and unpriced~\cite{pfleeger1997}.

\paragraph{The closest precedent.} Sobel and Clarkson compared formal and
informal development across student teams~\cite{sobel2002}. Berry and Tichy
characterise it as a quasi experiment --- ``assignment was voluntary and not
random'' --- and called for a complete redesign~\cite{berry2003}; the
authors' rebuttal argues theirs was field rather than laboratory
research~\cite{sobel2003}. Theirs is the closest prior comparison; it was not
randomised and it priced neither arm. Our sealed acceptance tests and
order-counterbalanced grading respond directly to that critique. The closest
work in unit of analysis reports 87 minutes to develop each of 73 unit proofs
against recreated defects~\cite{amusuo2025} --- per-artifact cost, but
against a detection-rate baseline, in human minutes rather than tokens.

\paragraph{Agentic repair, its cost, and its variance.} LLM agents repairing
real defects is established ground~\cite{jimenez2024,yang2024,xia2023}, as is
the finding that simpler structured pipelines can beat autonomous agents on
quality and dollars --- Agentless reports \$0.70 per issue~\cite{xia2024}.
Our contribution is not ``structure helps'' but \emph{which} structure, at
what price. Cost-aware agent evaluation is prior art~\cite{kapoor2024}, and
same-task token consumption can differ by up to $30\times$~\cite{bai2026}
--- a noise floor any cost delta must clear, and the documented form of the
unpredictability Goal~1 targets. Benchmark contamination motivated seeding
our own faults~\cite{aleithan2024}; agent repair evaluated inside Google
shows a large gap between machine-reported and human-reported
defects~\cite{rondon2025}. The nearest neighbour varies one prompt line
controlling specification grounding for $+38$ percentage points of correct
code~\cite{haeri2026}, but reports no cost accounting.

\paragraph{Review effectiveness.} Review surfaces far more maintainability
issues than defects: 75\% of review findings do not affect visible
functionality~\cite{mantyla2009}, replicated in open source with 7--35\% of
suggestions producing no change~\cite{beller2014}, anticipated by the
expectation--outcome gap~\cite{bacchelli2013}. Porter et al.\ found team size
and session count did not significantly move defect detection
rate~\cite{porter1998}; the per-review signal-to-noise figures we match are
Rigby and Bird's derivation from that data, and they report that more
reviewers yield ``only a minimal increase in the number of
comments''~\cite{rigby2013}. Our 85 findings without a defect in the fix is
therefore a replication. What appears unclaimed is the \emph{stopping rule}:
over 80\% of human changes involve at most one review
iteration~\cite{sadowski2018}, so twelve rounds under an unbounded
instruction is an order-of-magnitude departure.

\paragraph{Seeded faults --- a threat we inherit.} Mutants couple to 73\% of
real faults and 17\% of real faults couple to no mutant~\cite{just2014};
correlation with real-fault detection weakens once suite size is
controlled~\cite{papadakis2018}; uncontrolled mutation-based designs incur
Type~I errors $\sim$62\% of the time~\cite{papadakis2016}; hand-seeded faults
tend to be harder to detect than real ones~\cite{andrews2005}. Our defence is
that single-statement bugs are an abundant measured
class~\cite{karampatsis2020}.

\paragraph{Position bias in LLM grading.} Position bias is
established~\cite{zheng2023,wang2024}, strongest when the quality gap is
small~\cite{shi2025} --- exactly our regime. Our first grading round showed 4
of 4 pairs favouring the second-presented submission; on re-examination that
pattern is fully explained by a genuine arm effect under the pre-registered
parity alternation, so the honest statement is that our first design
\emph{could not have detected} position bias, not that we observed it. We
retain the counterbalanced design because it removes the confound we could
not otherwise rule out. The instrument's SE-specific validation is partial,
and the unvalidated part cuts against one of our own outputs: the source
validates LLM-as-judge for output-based \emph{direct scoring} on code
translation and generation, but concludes that current methods ``fail to
deliver satisfactory and consistent comparison performance on SE tasks''
under \emph{pairwise} comparison~\cite{wangj2025}. Our per-axis rubric
falls on the validated side of that line; the holistic pairwise verdict of
Sect.~\ref{sec:quality} falls on the side the source argues against --- a
source-backed reason, beyond its non-registration, to weight the
pre-registered axes over that verdict. For method comparison we follow standard
guidance~\cite{ko2015} and report effort as a separate dependent variable
rather than equalising it~\cite{hannay2009}.

\paragraph{What remains ours.} Searching for prior work pricing a
formal-methods workflow in LLM token cost against a conventional one returned
nothing we could verify; we state that as a searched-and-not-found result.

\section{Experimental Method}

\subsection{System, faults, execution}
The subject is a chess engine of 7{,}343 lines of hand-written Z80 assembly:
alpha--beta search, transposition tables, quiescence, Zobrist hashing,
built-in perft. An independent agent seeded seven single-fault variants, each
differing from pristine by \emph{exactly one byte} in the assembled binary,
wrote symptom-level reports with no location hints, and sealed an answer key
whose hash was published before any repair began. Each arm$\times$defect cell
ran as an isolated headless session on the same model at the same effort,
with no network access. FCDD's contract was built once as a separately
measured phase at \$21.75.

\subsection{An asymmetry we did not design and must disclose}\label{sec:asymmetry}

The contract was built by reading the \emph{pristine} engine, so Arm~B held a
derived oracle for correct behaviour that Arm~A never received; Arm~A's only
prior artefact was the symptom report. The asymmetry recurs in the
interpretation of every Goal-1 result below, because a ``consistent'' repair
guided by a pristine-derived oracle may be consistency purchased by the
oracle, not by the method's structure.

An earlier draft claimed the contract ``covers 7/7 faults by construction'';
that is false and withdrawn. Diffing each cell's specification against the
95-theorem baseline (re-counted mechanically for this revision; the counts
match) shows every Arm~B cell had to \emph{extend} the contract: bug07 $+12$,
bug02 $+16$, bug05 $+18$, bug01 $+29$, bug06 $+29$, bug04 $+33$, bug03 $+35$
theorems. The rule-layer clauses C1--C14 covered the rule-layer faults; the
search and evaluation layer was not covered and had to be specified from
scratch.

One case deserves its own report. The baseline contained an
evaluation-antisymmetry property, $\mathit{evalWhite}(\mathit{mirror}\ p) =
-\mathit{evalWhite}\ p$. A global sign inversion satisfies that identity
exactly --- the pristine-derived contract was \emph{vacuous} against bug04's
entire fault class, and the arm had to discover this and add
absolute-orientation anchors. That is a too-weak-specification result
obtained under controlled conditions, and it is evidence against the very
oracle advantage we are disclosing.

Two weaker mechanism statements survive re-derivation (an earlier draft's
grouping --- ``four cells needing new properties, median \$49.16 vs \$24.19''
--- could not be reproduced from the deposited artefacts and is withdrawn).
First, the number of theorems a cell added does \emph{not} predict its cost
(Spearman $\rho=0.03$; bug03 added the most theorems and was nearly the
cheapest). Second, the three faults seeded in the search/evaluation layer,
which the baseline did not cover, have a median cost of \$49.63 against
\$27.62 for the four rule-layer faults --- a mechanism consistent with
coverage-driven cost, not a tested effect ($n=7$, and one rule-layer cell
sits in the wrong group).

The fair design would have given Arm~A a cost-matched upfront artefact ---
roughly \$21.75 of characterisation tests built once from the pristine engine
--- and we did not. This advantages FCDD on localisation, and it would not
transfer to faults of omission, which lie outside any contract by definition.

\subsection{Measurement}
Cost is reported three ways, because they disagree and the disagreement
matters: the platform's figure; the same token counts priced with our
pre-registered rate table; and raw token totals. Token totals come from each
session's \texttt{modelUsage}, which includes reviewer subagents --- an
earlier ledger recorded the main-loop \texttt{usage} field only,
under-counting 22 rows by $1.1\times$--$82\times$ until repaired. Reviewer
invocations are counted mechanically from transcripts, because
(Sect.~\ref{sec:effort}) they covary with cost as strongly as method does.

\subsection{Pre-registration and its breaches}
The protocol was frozen before any run; changes are dated amendments
(A1--A10). Two breaches: \emph{rule 5 (replication) was honoured late} ---
bug01 triggers it (arm gap 10.8\%, under the 30\% trigger) and the previous
draft went to review with the rule undischarged; the replication has since
been completed and Sect.~\ref{sec:dispersion} reports it. And \emph{the
metric was substituted without amendment}: D3 froze a rate table and
legislated raw-only for unpriced models; the model used is unpriced there,
yet we reported platform dollars. Reverse-engineering the platform's rates
from 41 runs matches D3 within $\sim$6\%, so the substitution is materially
harmless --- but it was undocumented.

\section{Instrument Findings}\label{sec:instrument}

\subsection{Reviews that never closed}
Subagents defaulted to background execution and a headless session ends when
its main loop ends, so sessions terminated with reviews pending; five cells
self-reported waiting for reviewers. The stage was under-executed and
under-counted, symmetrically across arms.

\subsection{Unbounded review has no fixed point}
The corrected instruction read \emph{iterate until the reviewer has no
findings left}. That rule has no fixed point --- a competent reviewer
examining any test suite can always name one more uncovered case. In one cell
Arm~A fixed the seeded fault correctly on its first pass (one byte,
\texttt{cp WN} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{cp WP}), then ran \emph{12 review rounds
producing 85 findings, none of which was a defect in the fix}. Findings per
round: 7, 7, 5, 11, 8, 9, 5, 5, 5, 3, 8, 12 --- OLS slope $+0.025$/round,
$R^2=0.001$. No convergence. An earlier draft said we halted this run; the
record shows an \texttt{api\_error} 429 (session quota) and a second attempt
of \$67.94 our accounting missed. Summed per our own rule the cell cost at
least \$128.62 across 7.7 hours; the bounded re-run cost \$7.32 --- a
reduction of at least $17.6\times$.

\subsection{Cost covaries with review effort at least as strongly as with
method}\label{sec:effort}
An early draft claimed cost ``tracked reviewer count more tightly than which
arm was running'', from five cells in which arm and count were collinear ---
not identifiable. \emph{The regression published in the previous draft is
itself corrected here}: it printed $\mathrm{cost}=4.13+21.35\cdot\mathrm{ArmB}
+7.13\cdot\mathrm{rev}$, $R^2=0.826$; re-deriving from the deposited table
(13 final-instrument cells with a deposited platform cost) gives different
coefficients, and no subset of the deposited data reproduces those numbers.
The corrected fit (Fig.~\ref{fig:effort}):
\[
\mathrm{cost} = 1.56 + 16.85\cdot\mathrm{ArmB} + 8.76\cdot\mathrm{reviewers},
\quad R^2 = 0.873.
\]
Arm alone $R^2=0.580$ (gap \$27.90); reviewers alone $R^2=0.711$.
Controlling for reviewer count attenuates the arm gap from \$27.90 to
\$16.85: roughly 40\% of the measured method difference is attributable to
Arm~B invoking $1.8\times$ as many reviewers (mean 2.83 vs 1.57). The
correction \emph{strengthens} the section's claim --- reviewers alone now
explain more variance than arm alone --- but that is luck, not vindication: a
published regression failed to reproduce and we cannot reconstruct where its
coefficients came from.

A further correction, retained: bounding rounds did not compress Arm~A's
range to ``\$7--\$16'' as once claimed --- its costs are \$7.11--\$44.27, and
the outlier is the cell with three reviewers against \$8.66 with one.
Bounding rounds did not bound reviewer invocations, and cost followed the
latter.

\subsection{Killed processes emit no cost record}
One cell reported \$1.06; its first attempt had been killed without emitting
a result record. Recovering 34.67M tokens from the transcripts and pricing
them at our pre-registered rates gives \$23.98 (band \$23--\$33) --- an
under-count of $\sim$$20\times$ (an earlier draft said \$45.76 and
$43\times$, having borrowed a blended rate from a cell whose own denominator
was truncated). A second failure mode sits in our own ledger: one record
carries \$49.19 with all four token counters at zero. Exit reports can be
internally self-contradictory, not merely absent.

\subsection{Blinding defects in grading}\label{sec:blinding}
Our first grading round was voided: submissions were diffed against pristine,
but a correct minimal repair \emph{restores} pristine, so the repair was
invisible; FCDD's verification artefacts were omitted, so FCDD scored 1/5 for
testing on the grounds that it shipped no tests; fixing that leaked paths
naming the arm. \emph{The regraded round is not blinded, and we quantify
that rather than hedge it}: counting treatment-signature terms (theorem,
specification, clause, solver, witness, mutant) in one packet's two
submissions, at comparable length: 0 occurrences for the ordinary arm, 37
for FCDD. That is not a residue; it is a label. ``Blinded grading'' is
withdrawn as a competing-interest mitigation. The accurate description of
Sect.~\ref{sec:quality} is: \emph{unblinded graders preferred the submission
that visibly contains the treatment.} Compounding this, on three of seven
defects the source fixes were byte-identical, so the verdict turned entirely
on the visible artefact, and the test-quality axis mechanically rewards
shipping more verification material --- which is the treatment's definition.

\subsection{Cost (Goal 2)}\label{sec:cost}

Table~\ref{tab:cost}). \emph{FCDD was dearer on 7 of 7 pairs; exact
two-sided sign test $p=0.016$} --- the only statistically supportable result
in this study.

\paragraph{Headline metric.} Our pre-registration (D3) legislated for exactly
this model: \emph{``Opus 5 unpriced $\rightarrow$ raw-only if it ever
appears.''} Every measured row is that model. The primary figure is therefore
the raw-token median, $4.74\times$; platform dollars ($3.40\times$) and our
rate table ($3.28\times$) are robustness checks. Earlier drafts inverted this
--- headlining the metric D3 forbade, in a paper whose
Sect.~\ref{sec:instrument} proves platform self-reports here can be absent,
truncated, zero-token or self-contradictory. \emph{Interval:} an earlier
draft reported a ``98\% CI $[2.01,4.90]$''. That was an off-by-one in our own
\texttt{median\_ci} --- the true coverage of $[2.01,4.90]$ is 87.5\%; at
$n=7$ the tightest nonparametric interval reaching 95\% is the full observed
range, $[1.12, 6.88]$ (98.4\%). The bug was found by adversarial review of
the script, not by our own checks.

\paragraph{The 7/7 result depends on one author-computed imputation.} Taken
exactly as deposited, bug03/B is \$1.06 against Arm~A's \$11.96 --- Arm~B
\emph{cheaper} --- giving 6/7 and $p=0.125$. The 7/7 exists because we
recovered that cell's transcript tokens and priced them; no other cell
received transcript-level recovery, and no imputation rule was
pre-registered. Sensitivity: deposited values $\rightarrow$ 6/7, $p=0.125$;
imputed $\rightarrow$ 7/7, $p=0.016$; completing-attempt proxy (defined for 4
of 7 cells) $\rightarrow$ 4/4, $p=0.125$. The direction is robust across all
three; the significance is not. Estimator choice matters even more than
earlier drafts admitted, and we pre-specified none: median-of-ratios
$3.40\times$, ratio-of-medians $4.31\times$, ratio-of-totals $2.64\times$;
all reported. (The previous draft published 3.40 / 3.50 / 2.83 here; the
3.50 reproduces under no reconstruction we found, and the 2.83 matches the
superseded \$45.76 imputation.)

Per-pair gap: mean \$25.15, median \$22.18. \emph{No crossover exists in this
data} --- $N=\mathrm{upfront}/\mathrm{saving}$ is undefined when the saving
is negative on every pair. Our design cannot test the \emph{learning} half of
the pre-registered hypothesis at all: cells are memoryless by construction.

\subsection{Predictability (Goal 1a): dispersion, and what replication
showed}\label{sec:dispersion}

This is the section the method exists to win, so we hold it to the same
standard the cost section survived.

\paragraph{Across defects, the contrast rests on one cell.} With all seven
cells, ordinary development's cost spread is $6.23\times$ (CV 0.86) against
FCDD's $2.31\times$ (CV 0.33) --- the shape the thesis predicts. Excluding
bug01, the spreads are \emph{identical at $2.31\times$} (CVs 0.34 and 0.36).
A predictability claim whose entire evidentiary weight sits in one of seven
cells is not a claim about the method; it is a claim about that cell
(Fig.~\ref{fig:dispersion}a).

\paragraph{Within the one replicated cell, the arms differ in kind, not just
spread.} Our pre-registered rule~5 required replicating bug01 (arm gap
10.8\%); we ran three runs per arm under the final instrument
(Fig.~\ref{fig:dispersion}b). Arm~A produced two different solutions: a
\emph{minimal one-byte repair} restoring the pristine binary (\$11.78), or an
unrequested \emph{redesign of the quiescence policy} (\$44.27, \$41.35) ---
the same redesign two graders scored at or below the correctness gate floor
for an arithmetic underflow and a search-wide blast radius, and which the
sealed acceptance script passed identically. Arm~B produced the minimal,
byte-identical repair in \emph{all three runs} (\$49.63, \$79.28, \$51.40;
spread $1.60\times$, CV 0.28). Arm~A's cost is not scattered by noise; it is
determined by which strategy the run converges on (within-mode spreads
$1.07\times$ and --- including the instrument-v3 run, whose cost is
non-comparable but whose strategy datum is --- $1.36\times$; between modes
$3.8\times$). This is distinct from the $\sim$$30\times$ stochastic token
variance reported for agentic coding tasks~\cite{bai2026}: it is bimodal
strategy selection, interpretable rather than noise.

\paragraph{What this supports, stated exactly.} The strategy table (Arm~A: 1
minimal / 2 redesign; Arm~B: 3 / 0) gives Fisher exact $p=0.40$ (0.43
including the v3 strategy datum). At three runs per arm, \emph{no dispersion
statistic can reach significance} --- the best attainable two-sided $p$ in
any 3-vs-3 permutation test is 0.10. And the oracle asymmetry
(Sect.~\ref{sec:asymmetry}) offers a rival explanation requiring no method
effect at all: an arm holding a pristine-derived executable model may
converge on the pristine-restoring repair \emph{because it holds the model},
not because a contract disciplines its strategy. The honest classification:
\emph{a directional observation, consistent with the method's design intent,
on one defect, confounded by the oracle, and untestable at this replication
depth.} It is the right hypothesis for the next experiment
(Sect.~\ref{sec:future}); it is not a finding of this one.

\paragraph{Solution-matched cost, reported but not as an estimate.}
Restricting to runs where both arms produced the byte-identical repair leaves
three Arm~B runs against one Arm~A run (\$11.78); the three ratios are
$4.21\times$, $4.36\times$, $6.73\times$, quoted individually because a
denominator of one is not an estimator. The $1.12\times$ in
Table~\ref{tab:cost} --- the study's narrowest ratio --- compares FCDD's
minimal fix against Arm~A's expensive redesign; matched on solution it is
closer to the study's widest.

\paragraph{What the cost data adds to Goal 1.} If ``predictable'' includes
``you can budget for it'', the seven-pair table carries one more observation:
Arm~A's per-defect cost has a long right tail (six cells in \$7--\$17, one at
\$44), and the unbounded-review pathology shows what the tail looks like
without a stopping rule --- at least \$128.62 on a defect whose fix was one
byte and landed in the first pass. FCDD's band is narrower relative to its
own scale but sits three to five times higher.

\subsection{Quality and unfounded changes (Goal 1b)}\label{sec:quality}

The earlier comparison was withdrawn (order-confounded); we re-ran the
grading with \emph{each of the seven pairs graded twice, once in each
presentation order, by fourteen independent graders}. Presentation preference
came out exactly 7 first / 7 second, and all seven pairs returned the same
verdict in both orders --- evidence of grader reliability the single-rater
design could not provide.

\emph{FCDD was favoured on 6 of 7 pairs} ($p=0.125$). The paired composite
difference is $+0.17$ with a 95\% CI of $[-0.44,+0.78]$ --- the interval
spans zero, and the study is not powered to make a quality claim
(Fig.~\ref{fig:quality}). Two further caveats make even the directional
reading unsafe. First, the holistic preference item was never pre-registered
--- the protocol registers only the three 1--5 axes and the no-axis-below-3
gate --- and it contradicts the graders' own rubric totals on three of seven
pairs, all three toward FCDD; scored on the pre-registered instrument the
pairs split 3/2 with 2 ties, $p=1.00$. Second, the aggregate is carried
entirely by one pair: excluding bug01 the arms tie at exactly 4.50/4.50 and
correctness risk reverses to 4.67 (ordinary) against 4.42 (FCDD)
(Table~\ref{tab:quality}). The only axis surviving exclusion is test quality
--- which mechanically rewards shipping more verification material
(Sect.~\ref{sec:blinding}).

The advantage is concentrated in test quality and partly returned in clarity;
graders repeatedly found FCDD's evidence stronger but harder to read. On
three of the seven defects the two source fixes were byte-for-byte identical,
so the verdict turned entirely on verification. The defensible claim is
narrow: FCDD did not produce better \emph{fixes}; it produced better
\emph{evidence that the fix is right and will stay right} --- seeding each
fault as a permanent negative test, proving its detector fires by name, and
in three cases closing a stale-evidence false-green in which the gate had
been passing while the build's own self-test failed.

\paragraph{What this measures against the hallucination-reduction goal.}
Goal~1 claims the contract suppresses unfounded changes. The graded record
contains exactly one clear instance of the failure mode the goal targets:
Arm~A's bug01 redesign --- an unrequested rework of the quiescence policy
that introduced an arithmetic-underflow risk, scored 2 on correctness risk,
\emph{below the pre-registered gate floor of 3}, and passed the sealed
acceptance script anyway. FCDD produced no graded submission below the floor,
and on the replicated defect it never produced the redesign. That is the
design intent made visible --- once. Against it: both arms produced
beyond-minimal changes on other defects (Arm~A's bug06 extra was a genuine
latent-defect fix the grader valued; Arm~B's bug06 binary also differs from
pristine), the quality interval spans zero, and the one clean instance sits
on the same oracle-confounded cell as every other Goal-1 signal. \emph{This
experiment therefore does not establish hallucination reduction.} What it
establishes is narrower and still worth having: the sealed symptom-level gate
--- the industry's default arbiter --- \emph{cannot see the difference}
between a careful repair and a risky redesign, because both pass it. A method
that wants credit for suppressing unfounded changes must first deploy an
instrument able to detect them; the contract's clause-level diff is such an
instrument, and the acceptance script is not.

\subsection{Correctness and beyond-gate findings}\label{sec:beyond}
Every defect was repaired correctly by both arms under the final instrument,
confirmed by sealed acceptance scripts. This is weaker evidence than it
sounds: the scripts were written by the agent that injected the faults, from
the same understanding of the engine's semantics, so a shared misconception
is undetectable --- the identical common-mode residual the paper quotes for
FCDD and had not applied to its own instrument. Both arms produced findings
beyond the reported defect: FCDD systematically (including a real defect in
its own delivered package --- a gate measured against a stale binary), and
ordinary review found a latent transposition-table defect and an
unadjudicated terminal-position path. ``Finds more than it was asked to'' is
not FCDD-specific.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Goal 1: predictability --- designed for, not demonstrated}
The method's purpose receives, from this experiment, the verdict
\emph{consistent but unproven}. Every Goal-1 signal points the same way ---
the across-defect spread, the single-mode replication, the absence of any
FCDD submission below the gate floor, the graders' 6-of-7 preference --- and
not one survives its own caveat: the spread contrast lives in one cell; the
replication depth caps attainable significance at $p=0.10$; the gate-floor
instance is a single event; the preference interval spans zero; and the
oracle asymmetry predicts most of it with no method effect at all. Had we led
with Goal~1 and reported these as findings, we would be doing with
dispersions exactly what we criticise cost claims for doing with anecdotes.
What the study does contribute to Goal~1 is sharper than a null: it
identifies the phenomenon worth measuring (bimodal strategy selection, not
continuous cost noise), the instrument capable of measuring it
(solution-mode coding by binary diff against pristine), the confound to
remove (a cost-matched pristine-derived artefact for the control arm), and
the replication depth required. Section~\ref{sec:future} turns that into a
design.

\subsection{Goal 2: cost --- demonstrated, with one imputation caveat}
FCDD was more expensive on every one of seven matched faults, holding a free
pristine-derived oracle its competitor lacked, and it never amortised. The
direction survives every cost basis and sensitivity; the significance
($p=0.016$ vs $0.125$) rests on one transcript-recovery imputation we
performed on our own study. The overhead on this workload was three to five
times --- not the order of magnitude folklore sometimes suggests, and not the
near-zero that would make the method free to adopt. Since the contract never
amortised, the trade the thesis proposes is real only if Goal~1's product
exists; this study priced the premium without confirming the insurance pays
out.

\subsection{The measurement lessons}
The result most likely to transfer is neither ratio. This study produced six
confident, plausible, wrong findings, and every one was caught by a
mechanical check independent of the agent that produced it --- three by our
own gates and transcripts, two by adversarial review of the finished draft,
and one (the Sect.~\ref{sec:effort} regression) by the re-derivation
discipline this revision imposed. Several of the errors favoured the
conclusion we were drifting toward. The concrete lessons for measuring
agent-based development: reconcile self-reported cost against transcripts;
report reviewer invocation counts; control the review termination rule (it
moved one cell $17.6\times$); randomise presentation order in grading; and
re-derive every published number from the deposited artefacts before each
revision, because one of ours could not be.

\section{Threats to Validity}\label{sec:threats}

\emph{Sample size:} seven defects, $k=1$ except one cell at $k=3$, one
codebase, one language, one model.
\emph{Oracle asymmetry} (Sect.~\ref{sec:asymmetry}): FCDD received a
pristine-derived executable model; Arm~A received nothing comparable. It did
not cover the faults by construction (every cell extended it), but it is the
most consequential design flaw and supplies a method-free explanation for
every Goal-1 signal.
\emph{Instrument instability:} the prompt changed four times; only
final-version cells are compared. Version purity was achieved;
reproducibility was not demonstrated --- the one replicated cell spans
$3.8\times$ across runs within the same arm and instrument.
\emph{Interruption asymmetry:} from the deposited attempt records, 6 of 7
Arm~B and 2 of 7 Arm~A cells required a resume (previously published as
``every/none'', then ``5 of 7''; only this value derives from the ledger).
The completing-attempt proxy gives $1.14\times$, $1.54\times$, $3.95\times$,
$4.04\times$ (median $2.75\times$); it is a floor, not a bracket. One Arm~B
bug01 replicate (\$79.28) carries resumption overhead inside the Goal-1
dispersion data.
\emph{Review-budget asymmetry:} the bounded rule caps rounds, not
invocations, granting Arm~B $1.8\times$ the review effort.
\emph{Gate common-mode:} acceptance scripts were written by the
fault-injecting agent --- and Sect.~\ref{sec:quality} shows the gate passing
a below-floor redesign, so the weakness is demonstrated, not conjectured.
\emph{Grading:} label-blind, not treatment-blind (37:0 signature terms).
Counterbalancing removed order confounding, but each pair$\times$order was
judged once: order-consistency, not inter-rater reliability. Graders ran on a
different model from the arms and their reviewers --- an earlier draft
asserted a shared model family, which is wrong for this data.
\emph{Population:} single-byte seeded faults are individually localisable,
plausibly favouring the cheaper method; none is a fault of omission, the
class that lies outside any contract.
\emph{Target-language externality:} every cell repaired one language, and
an unusual one: we assume hand-written Z80 assembly is far sparser in LLM
training corpora than mainstream languages --- a plausible but
\emph{unverified} premise: the training composition of the models used here
is not public, and neither work cited below measures Z80's share. What the
literature does establish is the axis: multi-language benchmarks were built
expressly to expose the effect of language frequency~\cite{cassano2022},
and rebalancing the training distribution helps low-resource languages,
though the absolute effect is modest --- the headline 66.48\% is a
\emph{relative} pass@$k$ improvement; the underlying absolute pass@100
moves (4B model, natural vs.\ balanced) run from $-3.1$ points (C\#, which
regresses) to $+13.0$ (Julia), mean $\approx{+}6$~\cite{orlanski2023} ---
so the frequency axis is real but neither large in absolute terms nor
monotone. Two effects follow, with stateable expected signs. \emph{(i) Sparsity widens the unguided arm}: with
fewer memorised idioms to recall, more of an unconstrained session is search
rather than recall, so part of Arm~A's dispersion --- and therefore of the
Goal-1 contrast --- may be a property of Z80, not of the missing contract
(the sign holds for within-strategy wander; for strategy \emph{forking} it
is genuinely ambiguous, since a rich language also offers more memorised
redesigns to fork to). \emph{(ii) Closed semantics flatter the contract}:
Z80's fixed instruction set, absence of runtime and library surface, and
small machine state make a finite-scope contract unusually cheap to build
(\$21.75) and unusually binding; a large-runtime language would cost more to
specify and bind less. Jointly the expected direction differs by goal: a
mainstream-language replication should \emph{raise} the cost ratio (recall
cheapens Arm~A while specification surface raises Arm~B's contract side ---
our measured 3--5$\times$ is a plausible floor, consistent with
Sect.~\ref{sec:method}'s cheapest-tier argument) and \emph{shrink} the
dispersion contrast (a narrower unguided arm against a less binding
contract) --- on language grounds the cost finding is conservative and the
predictability observation anti-conservative. Effect \emph{(ii)} also
compounds the oracle asymmetry (Sect.~\ref{sec:asymmetry}) rather than
sitting orthogonal to it: a pristine-derived oracle is worth most exactly
where the model's prior knowledge is thinnest. None of this is testable
here --- language never varies across any cell; the nearest within-study
proxy, fault subsystem, is not language variation at all (for the record,
Arm~A's spread is $1.68\times$ within the four rule-layer cells against
$3.92\times$ within the three search/eval cells --- carried by bug01 again
and confounded with defect difficulty and contract coverage). The natural
language of prompts and reasoning --- the secondary reading of ``output
language'' --- was English throughout and identical across arms, so it
cannot contribute to a between-arm contrast. Item~(2) of
Sect.~\ref{sec:future} specifies the replication that would make the
language axis measurable.
\emph{Contamination:} the orchestrator read session self-reports while
ingesting results and unsealed the key at grading.
\emph{Conflict of interest:} see the declarations; an external audit found
eight discretionary choices, seven favouring the authors' own method.

\section{Future Work: Buying More Predictability}\label{sec:future}

The question this study leaves is what would make Goal~1 decidable, and what
each candidate improvement would buy per dollar.

\paragraph{(1) Power the predictability claim before making it.} The
phenomenon is identified: bimodal strategy selection, detectable mechanically
(binary diff against pristine classifies minimal-fix vs redesign at zero
marginal cost). A decidable design needs $k\geq 6$ runs per arm per defect on
at least three defects (at $k=3$ the attainable two-sided $p$ floors at 0.10;
at $k=6$ a clean 6/0-vs-3/3 strategy split reaches $p\approx 0.01$); a
pre-registered dispersion estimator (log-cost CV with a permutation test);
and the control arm equipped with a cost-matched pristine-derived artefact
($\approx$\$21.75 of characterisation tests) so the oracle confound is spent
symmetrically. At observed per-cell prices this is roughly
\$1{,}400--\$2{,}000 of runs --- comparable to this entire study, for one
decidable bit. That is the actual price of the predictability claim.

\paragraph{(2) A cross-language replication prices the language confound.}
The target-language threat (Sect.~\ref{sec:threats}) is untestable in this
design because the language being repaired never varies, and it bears on
the two goals with opposite signs --- it argues the cost ratio is a floor
and the dispersion contrast is inflated. The decisive experiment
manipulates language and holds everything else as fixed as a different
codebase permits: seed the same seven fault classes --- all
language-portable (an inverted guard, an unreset counter, a generation
mask, an evaluation sign, a rights-bookkeeping slip, a cache depth bound, a
terminal misclassification) --- into a chess engine of comparable scope in
at least one high-resource language (Python or C; compact open-source
engines exist in both), build the contract fresh for that engine (the
build cost is itself a primary measurement: effect \emph{(ii)} predicts it
rises with runtime and library surface), and run both arms under
item~(1)'s controls. At the measured per-cell means (\$15.33 Arm~A,
\$40.47 Arm~B) one added language costs ${\approx}\$390$ of runs at $k=1$
and ${\approx}\$1{,}170$ at $k=3$, plus contract build and seeding --- the
same order as item~(1), with which it shares runs if the $k\geq 6$ defects
are drawn from the new language. It can settle whether the unguided arm's
spread and strategy-forking rate move with training-data share
(effect~\emph{i}) and whether contract build and per-defect extension
costs track semantic surface (effect~\emph{ii}) --- each a signed
prediction stated before any data exist. It cannot separate language from
codebase beyond the fault-class matching, and it says nothing about faults
of omission or other model families. A third, mid-frequency language turns
the two-point contrast into a trend for roughly another \$1{,}200.

\paragraph{(3) Certified extraction: from sampling agreement to correctness
by construction.} Case~01's deepest structural weakness is that spec, twin
and bridge \emph{sample} each other's agreement. The Rocq/Coq route ---
specify the contract in Rocq, prove the properties deductively, and extract
executable OCaml from the proved specification, as CompCert does at
scale~\cite{leroy2009} --- replaces the twin-and-bridge beats with an
artefact correct by construction relative to its spec. For Goal~1 the buy is
qualitative: for covered properties the failure mode shifts from
\emph{detected downstream} to \emph{unrepresentable}, and the per-defect
verification cost for that class approaches zero --- precisely the regime in
which this study's never-amortising upfront cost could amortise; the
crossover arithmetic that Sect.~\ref{sec:cost} found undefined becomes
well-defined the moment one defect class's marginal cost drops below the
ordinary arm's. The cost: deductive proof effort well above the \$21.75
finite-scope tier we priced (a floor, per Sect.~\ref{sec:method}); and
extraction certifies the extracted core against \emph{its own spec} only ---
it does not certify the spec (bug04's vacuous antisymmetry clause would
survive extraction untouched), the FFI boundary, the numeric-width
remapping, or the parts left in Z80 and JavaScript. Those leaks are exactly
where ``extracted therefore correct'' fails in practice and must be budgeted
as first-class work.

\paragraph{(4) An independent oracle attacks the common-mode residual.} Both
the method's own disclaimer and our gate finding reduce to one sentence:
\emph{spec and checker share an author.} The cheapest structural attack is a
second, independently-authored specification to differential-test against.
One partially exists in the \emph{Chess.lean} project\footnote{%
\url{https://github.com/dwrensha/Chess.lean} --- a Lean~4 formalisation,
self-described as incomplete: board representation, move legality, turn
tracking, checkmate detection, oriented toward proving mate puzzles.
Verified 2026-07-30; re-verified 2026-08-06.}. Its rules/legality/mate layer
overlaps our contract's C1--C6/C11; the strategy layer our expensive cells
needed (search termination, eval anchoring, TT soundness) is out of its
scope. Highest-value use is therefore as an \emph{independent oracle}:
differential-test our spec's move generation and terminal decisions against
it --- days of encoding reconciliation for the first evidence about spec
correctness that does not pass through this study's single author.

\paragraph{(5) The Lean-to-Rocq import path is a spike, not a plan.} The
tempting composite --- reuse Chess.lean \emph{and} get certified extraction
via \emph{rocq-lean-import}\footnote{%
\url{https://github.com/rocq-community/rocq-lean-import} --- translates Lean
export files into Rocq declarations. Its own README (verified 2026-08-06)
labels it an experimental alpha, states the translation breaks extraction
for some constructs, requires conversion checking disabled for the Lean
stdlib, and reports mathlib intractable (11{,}867 of 66{,}400 entries
skipped even with errors tolerated).} --- is the path the tool's own
documentation says is most likely to fail. The correct next step is a
bounded spike ($\leq$1 session): does Chess.lean's dependency set survive
\texttt{lean4export} plus the importer, and does Rocq extraction produce
OCaml for the chess definitions specifically? Any red answer falls back to
(3)'s Rocq-native spec with Chess.lean kept in its oracle role.

\paragraph{(6) Cheap contract-side hardening from this study's own
failures.} \emph{Minimal-repair obligation}: make ``the smallest change
satisfying the violated clause'' an explicit, diff-size-budgeted contract
obligation --- targets the bug01 redesign fork directly at the cost of a
prompt line and a gate check; its risk is forbidding the beneficial
latent-defect fix Arm~A's bug06 run produced, so the organic-bonus lane must
remain a sanctioned exit. \emph{Specification mutation testing}: bug04
proved a clause can be vacuous against an entire fault class; systematically
mutating the twin and requiring each mutation to trip a clause would have
found that vacuity at build time. \emph{Invocation-capped review}: capping
invocations rather than rounds makes the review budget an actual budget
(Sect.~\ref{sec:effort}).

\paragraph{(7) Instrument the fix/verification split.} This study cannot say
what fraction of FCDD's 3--5$\times$ premium buys verification rather than
repair, because cells report only totals. Timestamping the moment the fix
first passes the acceptance script would split every cell into time-to-fix
and verification tail --- the number a practitioner budgeting FCDD adoption
actually needs, and the number that decides whether future effort should go
to cheaper proving or faster localisation.

\section{Conclusions}

(1) Both methods reliably repaired real assembly defects; every cell under
the final instrument produced an acceptance-passing fix.
(2) \emph{Goal 2 (cost) is answered.} FCDD was more expensive on 7 of 7
matched pairs ($p=0.016$, contingent on one disclosed imputation; direction
robust regardless), median ratio $4.74\times$ on raw tokens and
$\approx$3.3--3.4$\times$ on priced bases, interval $[1.12,6.88]$, and its
upfront contract never amortised.
(3) \emph{Goal 1 (predictability) is not answered, and we decline to pretend
otherwise.} The across-defect dispersion contrast rests on a single cell;
the replicated cell shows the predicted shape --- FCDD single-mode, the
ordinary arm forking between a minimal fix and a below-gate-floor redesign
--- at a depth where no test can reach significance (Fisher $p=0.40$;
attainable floor 0.10) and under an oracle asymmetry that predicts the same
shape without any method effect. It is a hypothesis with a mechanism and an
instrument, not a finding.
(4) \emph{No quality claim is made.} FCDD was favoured on 6 of 7 pairs
($p=0.125$) and the paired composite CI $[-0.44,+0.78]$ spans zero;
excluding one pair the arms tie exactly, and the grading was label-blind but
not treatment-blind. What survives is narrow: identical or equivalent fixes,
better \emph{evidence} --- and one demonstration that the sealed gate cannot
distinguish a careful repair from a risky redesign, which is the strongest
argument in this data for contract-shaped instruments even if not for the
contract's price.
(5) \emph{Measurement discipline dominates.} Report reviewer counts,
reconcile costs against transcripts, control the review termination rule,
randomise grading order, and re-derive every number before every draft ---
we failed each of these at least once, and each failure produced a
publishable-looking result that was false.

\paragraph{Data availability.} Deposited: the protocol with all amendments
(A1--A10), the per-cell ledger, the raw session records, the prompt packs,
the grading packets and verdicts, the contract, and the analysis and figure
scripts. Not deposited: the sealed answer key, the acceptance scripts, the
seeded variants, the subject engine and the emulator harness (a SHA-256
manifest of 206 files is deposited). The per-cell workspaces on which
reviewer counts, binary comparisons, gate verdicts and per-cell theorem
counts depend sit outside the repository, so those specific claims are not
independently checkable from the artefact.

\paragraph{AI usage.} This study measures AI agents and was conducted by
them: all sessions, both arms, all reviewers and all graders were LLM
agents; orchestration, analysis and this manuscript were produced by an LLM
agent under human direction. The human operator set the research question,
adjudicated amendments and directed each phase.
